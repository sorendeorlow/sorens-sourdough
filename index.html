Bagaren · The Baker

Meet Soren

Soren grew up in a Swedish home where bread was a daily ritual and a Sunday sacrament — a starter on the counter, rye in the oven on cold mornings, family gathered around a sliced loaf with butter, good cheese, and grace before the first bite.

That memory is the recipe behind every Communion Bread. No commercial yeast. No machine mixers. No shortcuts. Just stone-milled grain, a long-tended levain, the patience of a 36-hour bake — and a handwritten blessing card on every loaf.

"I bake the bread my grandmother would recognize — nothing more, nothing less."

— Soren

Det Äldsta Brödet · The Oldest Bread

The oldest meal in the book.

"Then Melchizedek, king of Salem, brought out bread and wine. He was priest of God Most High, and he blessed Abram."

— Genesis 14:18-19

Long before temple or law, a priest met a traveler on a road and brought out bread and wine. It became the pattern — Passover, the loaves and fishes, an upper room on a Thursday night, a kitchen table tonight. We bake in that line.
