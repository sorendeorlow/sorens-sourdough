Hantverket · The Craft

The process

A loaf of sourdough is built over two days. Here's how each one comes together — from a bubbling starter to a Dutch oven crackling in the heat.

01

The levain

It all starts with a starter — a living culture of wild yeast and bacteria we've been feeding for years. The evening before each bake, a portion is refreshed with fresh flour and water and left to double overnight.

No commercial yeast, ever. This is what gives our bread its tang, depth, and digestibility.

~12 hours

02

Mixing & autolyse

Flour and water rest together first — a quiet hour called the autolyse — letting the flour fully hydrate and gluten develop on its own. Then the levain and salt go in, mixed by hand.

No mixer. No machines. Just feel.

~1 hour

03

Bulk fermentation

The dough rests at room temperature for 4–6 hours with gentle stretch-and-folds every 30 minutes. This builds strength without overworking the gluten.

It's the slow part — and the most important part.

~4–6 hours

04

Shape & cold retard

Each loaf is shaped by hand and placed in a linen-lined banneton, then tucked into the fridge overnight for a long, cold fermentation.

This slow rest is where the deep, complex flavor comes from.

~12–18 hours

05

Score & bake

Each loaf gets a quick, confident score with a lame, then into a screaming-hot 6-quart cast iron Dutch oven. The lid traps steam — giving us that signature crackling, blistered crust.

Twenty minutes covered, twenty-five minutes uncovered, until the crust is a deep, caramel mahogany.

~45 minutes

06

Rest & cool

The hardest part. The loaf must rest for at least an hour to finish cooking from the inside out. You'll hear it sing — quiet crackles as the crust contracts.

Then it's yours.

~1 hour

Start to Finish

Roughly 36 hours per loaf

There's no shortcut. That's the point.
